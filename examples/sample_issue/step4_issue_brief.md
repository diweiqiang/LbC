## 2. Issue Brief

Forty years of empirical evidence from the Big Mac index show that exchange rates diverge stubbornly from purchasing-power parity, with developing currencies remaining heavily undervalued against the dollar even after adjusting for per-person income differentials [a02]. Yet attributing these imbalances to currency manipulation misunderstands global macroeconomics: bilateral trade deficits are produced by national gaps between savings and domestic investment rather than nominal exchange-rate choices [a01]. Artificially forcing a stronger yuan would merely redirect American import purchases to other low-cost exporters without shrinking America’s aggregate trade deficit, which is fueled by federal fiscal dissaving [a01][a02]. In Europe, where fiscal coordination remains similarly strained, the European Central Bank is preparing new banknote designs featuring natural landscapes and cultural figures to foster continental identity, but German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has firmly shut down any prospect of shared European safe assets, leaving capital markets fragmented [a49][a50]. Meanwhile, Japan is embarking on an ill-timed supplementary fiscal expansion under Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae that directly clashes with the Bank of Japan’s monetary normalization, further weakening the yen and exacerbating domestic inflation pressures [a51].

The physical and financial realities of technological progress are colliding across the global digital economy [a35][a47]. Big Tech hyperscalers are spending more than $250bn annually on artificial-intelligence infrastructure, yet end-user software revenues remain an order of magnitude smaller than this capital outlay, creating intense market skepticism regarding capital returns [a47]. In Britain, this infrastructure build-out is encountering severe physical limits, as planning restrictions in the green belt and a seven-year queue for National Grid electricity connections have forced technology firms to freeze $15bn in planned data-centre construction [a35]. Despite high-level capital scrutiny and infrastructure gridlock, practical adoption is accelerating at the grassroots, where American solopreneurs and small enterprises are harnessing generative AI agents to automate legal drafting, marketing, and accounting workflows, boosting one-person business productivity [a39]. Frontier research reveals that artificial intelligence and experimental quantum computing are developing a complementary symbiosis, with classical machine-learning algorithms optimizing the real-time error correction required for fragile physical qubits [a54][a55]. However, the flood of machine-generated prose has triggered an escalating arms race with stylometric forensic tools that analyze perplexity and structural burstiness to detect synthetic text, while advances in desktop gene synthesis lower the technical barrier to catastrophic engineered viral pathogens [a56][a58].

Geopolitical alliances are undergoing rapid reassessment as the prospect of American foreign-policy retrenchment shakes traditional defense arrangements across Europe, Asia, and North America [a03][a13][a18]. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has adapted to Donald Trump’s transactional worldview by offering partnerships in domestic drone production and natural-resource extraction to protect vital military support [a13]. In Ottawa, mounting anxiety over American isolationism has pushed Canada to break with sole-source US military procurement, awarding multi-billion-dollar submarine contracts to European and allied naval shipyards [a18]. European defense planners in Paris and Berlin are quietly exploring whether France's sovereign force de frappe of 290 nuclear warheads could provide an autonomous continental deterrence shield if Washington abandons NATO commitments, though historical German fiscal reluctance and the domestic political threat of a Marine Le Pen presidency leave that umbrella deeply uncertain [a38].
